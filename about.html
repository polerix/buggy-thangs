Our story

Felted by hand since 1994.

Buggy-Thangs started with one stop-motion puppet, a bag of raw wool, and a very sleepy bug.

In 1994, puppet-maker Wendell Fitch was building characters for Bugwood Hollow, a stop-motion kids' show about a family of bugs who lived under a porch. The pilot was shot over one long winter and never aired.

Wendell kept the puppets. Friends kept asking for them. So he started felting new ones at his kitchen table: chunky mossy bodies, knit eyestalks, scraps of old jeans for wings, and tiny boots so they could stand up on the shelf. The first one was Muffi.

The studio shelf, this morning. Nobody posed them.

The studio

Today the studio is an old parish hall outside town, with long felting tables, a wall of wool, and a shelf for every character in the line. We work small batches, mostly in the evenings. Wendell always said they come out better at night.

Why was Bugwood Hollow shelved? Officially, budget. The crew remembers it differently: on the last night of the shoot, every puppet on the set had turned to face the camera. Nobody had touched them.
