Our story

Felted by hand since 1994.

Buggy-Thangs started with one stop-motion puppet, a bag of raw wool, and a very sleepy bug.

In 1994, puppet-maker Wendell Fitch was building characters for Bugwood Hollow, a stop-motion kids' show about a family of bugs who lived under a porch. The pilot was shot over one long winter and never aired.

Wendell kept the puppets. Friends kept asking for them. So he started felting new ones at his kitchen table: chunky mossy bodies, knit eyestalks, scraps of old jeans for wings, and tiny boots so they could stand up on the shelf. The first one was Muffi.

The studio shelf, this morning. Nobody posed them.

Why it never aired

Why was Bugwood Hollow shelved? Officially, budget. The crew remembers it differently: on the last night of the shoot, every puppet on the set had turned to face the camera. Nobody had touched them.

Modern Slavery Statement

Last reviewed: 1994. Reviewed again: tonight.

Introduction

At WENDELL FITCH PRODUCTION we are committed to ethical business practices and to ensuring that modern slavery, forced labour, and human trafficking have no place in our operations. Our operations take place in a sealed parish hall after dark, so we are confident they have no place to go. We recognize our responsibility to uphold human rights, and we have carefully confirmed that none of our workers is, technically, human.

Our Commitment to Ethical Sourcing

We source our materials and products from carefully selected suppliers who adhere to fair labour practices. Our commitment includes:

  • Working with artisans who receive fair wages, safe working conditions, and respect for their rights. Wages are paid in wool, by the gram, and spent on more wool.
  • Ensuring that no child labour, forced labour, or human trafficking occurs within our supply chain. Every worker is at least thirty years old and arrives on its own six legs.
  • Conducting due diligence to verify the ethical standards of our suppliers. We knocked. Something knocked back.

Our Supply Chain and Partnerships

WENDELL FITCH PRODUCTION collaborates with suppliers who meet high ethical standards. We ensure that:

  • Our manufacturing partners comply with international labour laws and industry best practices in every jurisdiction that recognizes felt as a legal person. There are none, which keeps the paperwork light.
  • We prioritize suppliers certified by the Bugwood Hollow Ethical Felting Board, founded, chaired and staffed by Wendell Fitch. The Board has not met since October 2019.

Policies and Monitoring

We have implemented policies to safeguard against modern slavery, including:

  • A Supplier Code of Conduct, pinned to the inside of the studio door. Nobody inside has been able to read it. The lights are off.
  • Regular reviews and assessments of our supply chain to identify and address potential risks. The night shift reviews us, too.

Ongoing Improvements

WENDELL FITCH PRODUCTION is dedicated to continuously improving our approach to ethical sourcing. We will:

  • Conduct periodic audits and reviews of our supply chain, in daylight, in pairs.
  • Regularly visit our supplier beneath the parish hall. Our last auditor is still visiting.

Contact Us

If you have any questions about our commitment to ethical labour and modern slavery prevention, please contact WENDELL FITCH PRODUCTION. Wendell is on leave. Please do not contact the night shift. It will contact you.

Signed on behalf of the Board, W. Fitch

Wendell stopped coming in on October 31st, 2019.

His felting needle was still stuck in the first Muffi. It was facing the door.
